Prompts and Inspirations: From The Writer's Desk

Reading Prompts

  1. Steal a Sentence

    Copy a sentence from something you're reading and mark the word or phrase that stands out most. Underneath, add a thought, doodle, or short response explaining what made you want to keep it.

  2. Word Scavenger Hunt

    Find five words while reading that sound strange, unfamiliar, or interesting to you. Scatter them across your page and surround them with definitions, drawings, colors, or associations.

  3. Build a Character's Pocket

    Choose a character and imagine five objects they might carry in their pocket, bag, or room. Draw or list the objects and add a quick note about why one or two belong to them.

  4. Reading Collage

    Gather a quote, a word, a thought, and a small drawing inspired by your current reading. Arrange everything together however you'd like and let the different pieces share the page.

Art Prompts

  1. Draw What You Kept

    Find a receipt, wrapper, ticket, label, leaf, or other small scrap and attach it to your page. Draw, write, or add color around it until the ordinary object becomes part of something new.

  2. 5-Minute Page

    Set a timer for five minutes and fill the page with anything that comes to mind: doodles, words, patterns, scraps, or colors. When the timer ends, stop wherever you are and leave the page unfinished.

  3. Illustrate a Thought

    Write the first thought that comes into your head, no matter how random it seems. Respond using colors, shapes, symbols, patterns, or doodles instead of trying to explain it with more words.

  4. Color of the Moment

    Choose three colors that remind you of a place, memory, book, song, or moment. Make a small pattern or design using only those colors.

Writer Prompts

  1. Capture a Moment

    Write 3–5 sentences about one small moment from today that you don't want to forget.

  2. Keep the Unfinished Thought

    Write down a question or idea that's been sitting in your head and explore it for a few sentences. Leave some empty space underneath so you can return and add another thought whenever one appears.

  3. Overheard

    Record an interesting phrase or sentence you heard recently and write a few lines about what it made you think about. It could lead to a memory, question, observation, or completely unrelated idea.

  4. Make an Unimportant List

    Choose a strangely specific topic, such as sounds I heard today, things on my desk, or things that are blue, and start a list. Leave room to keep adding to it whenever you notice something new.
